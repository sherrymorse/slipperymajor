Slippery Nipple League 2026 (Major), Week 9: Keeping this TRAIN Going…

Posted by the Commissioner · Sunday, November 8, 2026

Well, after a week off to observe the slut-tastic holiday that has become our modern Halloween — we’re back in the saddle, and it’s great to be back on the grind.

Sometimes in life, temptation creeps in to perhaps get a little lax with certain things. But in my opinion, we must actively resist it, because that’s a recipe for spoiled-rottenness and taking the good things in life for granted.

The truth is, anything worth having is something that needs to be nurtured and cared for — and not everything has the energy to sustain itself. So we’re called to identify those things, lead by example and nurture them.

Think of it as taking care of the things that take care of us. And I can’t speak for everyone, but I don’t want to think about how bored I would be in a life without fantasy football. That applies no matter what else I had going on, or how busy I was or wasn’t.

And so here we are, slightly past the midway point of this thing, ready to take it down the home stretch before we call it another season — and then eventually head into the long and dreary wasteland we call the offseason. Each year, we promise ourselves we’ll savor the moment and embrace each and every week of the season.

And that’s what life is all about — taking time to smell the roses and not treating things we love like they’ll always be there. Time is fleeting. So with that in mind, and as things begin to get super tight, just like prom night in this bitch — we embrace the grind and keep this TRAIN going on into Week 9.

Onto this week’s predictions:

Scott’s trade was understandable, and it could pay dividends — but it’s risky. Fact is, time is of the essence right now for Mr. Wood. Derrick Henry hasn’t been full Derrick in about a month now, so he’s putting up RB2/RB3 production with an RB1 cost of entry. Until this trade, Scott simply didn’t have a reliable RB2. And he definitely has one now — but he can’t use him this week.

So this begs the question: how much will it matter if he drops to (2-7)? The ability to stream two above-average QBs is definitely an every-week upgrade at the position — if he can get the start/sit decisions right most weeks. But at (2-6), the only thing that matters is the here and now.

Binh is having a fine season so far, but he didn’t really take advantage of Henry’s lackluster game with the Zay Flowers start. So Scott’s in this one for sure — but would I bet on it?

Well, the way I see it, this one’s a true 60/40. One unexpected performance could flip this one in Scott’s favor, but as things stand, I like Binh’s team to take this one and advance to (6-3).

This is a get-right game for one of these two gents. Two weeks ago, Daniel was (6-0), and the landscape was such that it was almost time to start having conversations about what sandals Daniel was going to wear on the beach while sipping Raising Cane’s iced tea in Round 1 of the playoffs. With two straight losses, those conversations will have to wait a bit, because don’t look now — but the rest of the field is starting to catch up.

Well, he’s done that and then some — so it’s kind of a head-scratcher as to how it could be that Mark has dropped four of his last five after the (2-1) start.

Some of it is injuries. Some of it is that certain players who started out red-hot have since cooled down. And I think you can chalk the rest up to bad luck.

All of that might truly be behind Mark, though, with many of his players having amazing matchups this weekend. Will it be enough? Daniel still has an amazing team, in spite of his recent stumble. I’m not sure, honestly. This is one of the tougher games to call on the slate this weekend.

I definitely think conditions are ripe for a third straight upset against Daniel, and this one will be high-scoring — but in the end, I think Daniel does just enough to keep Mark’s excellent team at bay and stop his slide. Daniel wins this one by the skin of his chinny-chin-chin.

You figured he would make some kind of push at some point, but it’s hard to see four straight wins coming directly after four straight losses, no matter how capable the fantasy owner is who finds himself in such a situation.

And while A Tale of Two Cities is mandatory reading at Rummel, it’s been a tale of two different seasons for Zack, as suddenly his once-downtrodden team is now ripe for the Best of Times after withstanding the onslaught of the Worst of Times in the first half of the season.

And while we’re just one game apart at this point, it’s been a little bit of a different path for the commish to arrive at (5-3). After dropping my first game in a tough loss to David’s invincible Week 1 squad, it’s been mostly smooth sailing since.

But make no mistake: this is a tough game for both of us. Zack conceded as much when somehow he was able to talk someone who is (2-6) into investing in his bye-week-laden asset, which allowed him to inject much-needed points into the starting lineup.

So will it pay off? This one is going to be a reeeally close one, but it will all hinge on whether Josh Allen can cut through the tough matchup and put up his usual 30+. If he does, Commish takes it. I never bet against Josh.

Commish squeaks by, by less than 5 points.
